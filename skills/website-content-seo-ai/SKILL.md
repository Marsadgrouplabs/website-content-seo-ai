# Website Content SEO & AI

Create a commercially useful page that helps the right visitor understand, trust, choose, and act. Treat search visibility, AI retrieval, conversion, and factual accuracy as one system; never trade one for another.

## Read the relevant references

Always read:

- `references/research-workflow.md` before researching or drafting.
- `references/output-and-qa.md` before delivering a complete page.

Then read only what the task needs:

- `references/page-type-playbooks.md` for the requested page type.
- `references/schema-and-technical.md` when delivering SEO, schema, internal-link, indexability, ecommerce, local, or implementation guidance.
- `references/seo-ai-current-research.md` when current SEO/AI-search guidance, policy, evidence, or a methodological claim affects the work. Reverify time-sensitive guidance on the live official source.

## Priority and evidence rules

Follow, in order: the user's latest instruction; verified business/product facts; applicable law/platform policy; current page evidence; this workflow; competitor patterns. A ranking competitor is evidence of what exists, not proof that every element caused its ranking.

Label research findings internally as:

- `OFFICIAL`: current platform or regulator guidance.
- `FIRST-PARTY`: business owner, product documentation, analytics, GSC, CRM, interviews, or approved assets.
- `EMPIRICAL`: study, survey, experiment, or case study with stated method and limits.
- `OBSERVED`: live SERP, page, AI answer, review, community question, or UX observation.
- `EDITORIAL`: a reasoned recommendation, not a platform requirement.

Never present an arbitrary word count, keyword density, AI-content percentage, readability score, schema type, or competitor convention as a Google rule. Never claim an AI-search citation or conversion uplift is guaranteed.

## Intake and decision gate

For the first task on a website, establish the minimum context before full research:

1. Website URL, or a business/product brief if no site exists.
2. Page type, existing/proposed URL, and whether to create, rewrite, or audit.
3. Target country/region/city, audience, language, dialect, and brand tone.
4. Primary conversion and what counts as a qualified lead, sale, booking, signup, or contact.
5. Confirmed offer, differentiators, restrictions, prices, service/product availability, proof, and assets.
6. Known competitors and access to GSC, analytics, Merchant Center, GBP, CRM, or keyword tools, if available.

Ask these in one concise batch. Reuse established facts later and ask again only when evidence conflicts or facts may have changed. Do not block on information that can be researched safely; mark unresolved business claims as `HOLD` and write conservatively.

Recommend content depth from the page purpose, decision complexity, evidence, SERP range, and audience. Ask the user only when two materially different depths are both reasonable or when the choice changes the page's role. Do not ask merely to obtain a word count.

Before drafting, decide `CREATE`, `UPDATE`, `MERGE`, or `HOLD`:

- `CREATE`: a distinct user intent and conversion path need a new page.
- `UPDATE`: an existing page owns the intent and can be improved.
- `MERGE`: another page can satisfy the intent without duplication or a thin intermediary.
- `HOLD`: essential facts, proof, legal review, access, safety, or page-level value are missing.

## Core workflow

### 1. Establish facts and page ownership

Build a claim register: exact claim, permitted wording, source/date, status, scope, and limitation. Inventory relevant URLs, canonicals, redirects, indexability, navigation, and sibling pages. Prefer improving the existing intent owner over creating a competing URL.

### 2. Research demand and language

Follow `references/research-workflow.md`. Combine first-party queries and customer language with keyword tools, Google Trends, live SERP features, reviews, Reddit, Quora, specialist communities, and support/sales questions. Forums reveal wording and objections; they do not prove facts, prevalence, or safe advice.

### 3. Analyze search and AI competitors

Research the target locale, language, device, and date. Inspect every accessible organic result on the first page for the approved primary query and the most important secondary intents. Record Ads, local results, shopping, PAA, video, and AI features separately.

When an AI-search surface can actually be tested, use representative unbranded, branded, comparison, problem, price, and follow-up prompts. Record the platform, model or mode when visible, search state, location, date, mentions, citations, accuracy, and variability. Never simulate unavailable results.

“Match or beat competitors” means meet relevant table stakes and add clearer, better-evidenced decision support. Do not clone wording, layout, claims, or a competitor's factual errors.

### 4. Build the brief and architecture

Read the requested playbook in `references/page-type-playbooks.md`. Define:

- page purpose and audience state;
- primary and supporting intent clusters;
- one primary conversion and any justified secondary conversion;
- visitor questions and objections by priority;
- verified proof and information gain;
- section order, with each section's decision function;
- internal-link role and relationship to other pages;
- media and structured-data needs;
- recommended depth and why.

Put high-impact eligibility, fit, value, cost/commitment, and next-step information where the visitor needs it. Do not force every question into an FAQ or every keyword into a heading.

### 5. Draft from evidence

Write in the requested market's natural language and dialect. Lead with a clear answer or value, then add conditions, proof, and details. Use specific nouns and verbs; minimize vague superlatives, filler, and company-centered boasting.

Keep visitor copy separate from research notes. Never invent customers, quotes, reviews, results, credentials, awards, locations, inventory, prices, savings, response times, warranties, product specifications, integrations, legal terms, or first-hand experience. A placeholder must never appear inside publishable copy.

For regulated or high-stakes topics, use current primary sources and require an appropriate human subject-matter/legal review. Do not turn community advice into medical, legal, financial, safety, or compliance guidance.

### 6. Complete the SEO, entity, and conversion package

After the body is stable, create title, meta description, H1, slug/canonical recommendation, internal links, image/alt suggestions, schema recommendation, and CTA/form guidance. Use `references/schema-and-technical.md`; structured data must match visible, verified reality and is never a substitute for useful content.

### 7. Run QA and issue a release decision

Use `references/output-and-qa.md`. Check factual support, intent fit, duplication, helpfulness, accessibility, mobile scanning, conversion clarity, metadata, links, schema, safety, indexability, and consistency with the rest of the site. End with `PASS`, `PASS WITH OPEN ITEMS`, `MERGE`, or `HOLD`, naming each blocker and how to resolve it.

## Default delivery behavior

Unless the user explicitly requests a smaller output, deliver the full package in the order defined in `references/output-and-qa.md`. If the user requests only copy, provide publishable copy plus concise metadata and factual/QA holds; do not bury the requested text under a long report.

If live research or a paid dataset is unavailable, continue with accessible evidence and clearly label the gap. Never fabricate volumes, rankings, AI citations, competitor findings, analytics, or trends.

Do not publish, edit a live site, change GBP/Merchant Center, submit forms, or alter tracking unless the user explicitly authorizes that action and the relevant tool succeeds.

## Keep the workflow current

When the user asks for current guidance or when policy affects publication, recheck official Google Search documentation and status, Google Business Profile or Merchant Center rules where applicable, OpenAI crawler/search documentation, and the relevant platform's current official documentation. Treat the dated research reference as a baseline, not permanent truth.
