# Commercial Page-Type Playbooks

Choose the requested page type and adapt it to the actual user journey. These are decision frameworks, not rigid templates. Omit any section without useful, verified content.

## Contents

1. Homepage

4. Service

7. Category or collection

## 1. Homepage

### Job

Orient mixed audiences quickly: identify the entity, what it offers, for whom, where/how it operates, why it is credible, and the best next path. A homepage is not a compressed version of every page.

### Research emphasis

- branded and category queries, sitelinks, entity confusion, and homepage query data;
- direct competitors' value propositions and navigation paths;
- the most commercially important services/products and audience segments;
- first-party brand language, sales objections, proof, and geographic scope.

### Default architecture

1. Clear category/value statement: business/product + audience + useful outcome.
2. Primary CTA and justified secondary path.
3. Main offers or pathways, written from visitor goals.
4. Differentiators supported by evidence.
5. Relevant proof: customers, work, reviews, data, credentials, or demonstrations.
6. How it works or what happens next.
7. Audience/use-case/location pathways.
8. Final CTA and contact reassurance.

### Conversion rules

Do not force all visitors into one high-commitment action when the audience includes earlier-stage evaluators. Make the primary action unmistakable; use secondary actions for genuine alternative stages, not decorative button clutter.

### Common failures

Vague slogans, unclear category, listing every capability, self-congratulatory copy, unsupported superlatives, rotating carousels hiding the main message, and a hero that never states who the company helps.

## 2. About

### Job

Help people verify the organization behind the offer: what it does, whom it serves, why it exists, how it works, where it operates, and what evidence supports its story. Support entity understanding without turning the page into a keyword dump.

### Research emphasis

- exact legal/trading names, founding facts, location, leadership, ownership where public, and operating scope;
- reasons visitors inspect About: trust, employment, partnership, press, procurement, regulation, or purchase reassurance;
- competitor positioning and entity ambiguity;
- approved milestones, expertise, values in action, community work, and authentic imagery.

### Default architecture

1. Short plain-language summary of what the organization does and for whom.
2. Origin or reason for existence, using verified dates and people.
3. Operating model, approach, or principles demonstrated through actions.
4. Leadership/team/expertise where it helps trust and can be verified.
5. Locations, markets, and audiences accurately distinguished.
6. Evidence: milestones, customers, work, certifications, press, or community actions.
7. Relevant path to services/products/careers/contact.

### Conversion rules

Choose the CTA by visitor context: explore services, meet the team, view work, partner, careers, press contact, or general contact. The page should reduce uncertainty before it asks for action.

### Common failures

Generic mission language, invented founder drama, unverifiable “years of combined experience,” stock photos presented as the team, values without examples, hidden contact/location facts, and a history longer than the decision requires.

## 3. Contact

### Job

Remove friction between intent and a successful contact. Tell users which channel fits, when it is available, what information is needed, and what happens after they reach out.

### Research emphasis

- actual phone, email, address, departments, service areas, hours, emergency/support boundaries, accessibility, and response process;
- contact reasons and routing needs;
- form completion/drop-off data and support/sales feedback;
- privacy and consent requirements in the target market.

### Default architecture

1. Direct contact heading and response expectation.
2. Primary phone/email/form plus purpose-specific channels if real.
3. Short form with only fields needed for the next step.
4. Physical address, directions, parking/accessibility, or service-area clarification as applicable.
5. Hours, time zone, emergency/after-hours path, and unavailable-channel caveats.
6. What happens after submission and alternative route if urgent.
7. Small set of contact-specific questions only.

### Conversion rules

Do not hide all contact options behind a form. Use descriptive submit text such as `Request a consultation` or `Send service details`, not a misleading promise. State expected response only if operations can meet it. Validate success/error messages and accessible labels.

### Common failures

Form-only pages, fake chat availability, incorrect map/address, service-area businesses implying a storefront, excessive required fields, vague `Submit`, no response expectation, and collecting sensitive data unnecessarily.

## 4. Service

### Job

Help the right prospect decide whether the service fits the problem, understand scope and process, assess evidence and risk, and take the next step.

### Research emphasis

- service/problem/outcome/use-case/local/comparison/cost queries;
- actual scope, exclusions, delivery model, prerequisites, timeline factors, price factors, and handoffs;
- top organic service pages and relevant AI answers;
- sales calls, proposals, reviews, failed alternatives, and decision objections;
- regulated or safety requirements from primary sources.

### Default architecture

1. Service + audience/problem + realistic outcome + CTA.
2. Fit/eligibility: who and what the service is for.
3. Problems/use cases handled and boundaries.
4. Deliverables or scope, written concretely.
5. Process and what the client must provide.
6. Evidence: relevant work, team, methods, reviews, or metrics.
7. Timing and price/cost factors without invented guarantees.
8. Why choose this provider, tied to proof.
9. Objection-resolving answers and exclusions.
10. Final CTA with the next step.

### Conversion rules

Urgent services put eligibility and contact high. Consultative/high-ticket services provide enough process, proof, and fit information before a call. Offer a lower-commitment action only when it supports the real sales process.

### Common failures

Dictionary definitions, benefit claims without scope, listing tools instead of outcomes, hiding exclusions, fake urgency, fixed delivery or result promises, testimonial fabrication, and duplicating multiple service/location combinations with no independent value.

## 5. Ecommerce product

### Job

Enable a confident purchase of one product or its legitimate variants by making fit, condition, total commitment, availability, and risk clear.

### Research emphasis

- product, model, attribute, use-case, comparison, compatibility, size, material, shipping, returns, and review queries;
- Merchant Center/feed/site consistency;
- marketplace and direct-merchant SERPs, Shopping, Images, Lens, and AI product answers;
- onsite search, reviews, returns, support tickets, and variant-selection problems;
- current price, currency, inventory, shipping, returns, warranty, safety, and regulatory facts.

### Default architecture

1. Exact product name/model, concise differentiator, current price/availability, variant selector, and purchase CTA.
2. High-quality media showing scale, details, use, and variants truthfully.
3. Key benefits linked to verifiable features/specifications.
4. Fit, compatibility, size, material, care, ingredients, or technical data as relevant.
5. Shipping cost/estimate, delivery timing basis, returns, warranty, and total-cost cues.
6. Use cases, setup, what is included, and exclusions.
7. Authentic reviews/Q&A and responses where available.
8. Comparison or selection help without false disparagement.
9. Support, safety, compliance, and final purchase reassurance.

### Conversion rules

Do not make users add to cart merely to discover a material charge or policy. Keep price, availability, selection, and CTA synchronized. Surface return and shipping information near the decision, not only in a global footer.

### SEO/schema notes

Use current `Product`/`Offer` merchant-listing guidance only for a purchasable single product or legitimate variants. Keep visible content, structured data, and feed aligned. Do not put single-product rich-result markup on a category list. Follow current AI-generated product-data and image metadata requirements when applicable.

### Common failures

Manufacturer-copy duplication, invented specs, fake scarcity, inaccessible variant states, hidden total cost, generic alt text, unverified ratings, review gating, mismatched feed/schema/visible price, and AI-generated images represented as the real product.

## 6. Lead-generation or non-ecommerce product

### Job

Explain what the product is, who benefits, what it does, how it fits existing workflows, and what evaluation or purchase step follows. Examples include SaaS, equipment sold by quote, platforms, programs, and B2B solutions.

### Research emphasis

- category, use-case, problem, feature, integration, alternative, comparison, demo, and pricing queries;
- buyer/user roles, implementation, requirements, security, support, and procurement questions;
- product documentation, roadmap boundaries, demos, trials, case studies, and verified integrations;
- competitors' category positioning and proof.

### Default architecture

1. Product category + target user + outcome + demo/trial/contact CTA.
2. Use cases and qualification.
3. Core capabilities explained as tasks/outcomes, not a feature wall.
4. Demonstration, screenshots, workflow, or architecture.
5. Integrations, requirements, implementation, support, security/compliance where relevant.
6. Evidence: named customers with permission, case studies, data, or product documentation.
7. Pricing approach or transparent reason for quote-based pricing.
8. Comparison, limitations, and next step.

### Conversion rules

Match CTA commitment to buying stage. A technical evaluator may need documentation; a buyer may need a demo or pricing; an end user may need a trial. Avoid forcing all roles to “Contact sales.”

### Common failures

Undefined category, feature jargon, fake customer logos, unsupported integrations, hiding implementation burden, vague AI claims, no product evidence, and claiming a roadmap item as available.

## 7. Category or collection

### Job

Help users understand a group, narrow choices, compare meaningful attributes, and reach every indexable item. The page is a selection tool, not a long article pasted above products.

### Research emphasis

- category, attribute, use-case, audience, brand, material, size, compatibility, and “best for” queries;
- filter and sorting usage, onsite searches, zero-result queries, and product coverage;
- category/list competitors, marketplaces, SERP composition, and faceted navigation;
- product-card information needed before clicking.

### Default architecture

1. Clear category H1 and concise selection context.
2. Useful subcategories, filters, sorting, and result count.
3. Product/service cards with decision-critical attributes.
4. Short buying/selection guidance near the relevant decision point.
5. Related categories and breadcrumbs.
6. Optional deeper guidance below results only when useful.

### Conversion rules

Make filters match user language and product differences. Preserve context when sorting/filtering. Ensure users can see enough attributes and imagery to choose which item deserves a click.

### SEO/technical notes

Use crawlable links from category to subcategory/product pages. Decide which facets are indexable; prevent uncontrolled parameter combinations from creating crawl waste or duplicates. Use pagination/incremental-loading patterns that still expose crawlable URLs. Do not use single-product merchant markup on the category itself.

### Common failures

SEO text that delays products, empty or near-empty categories, vague cards, inaccessible filters, infinite scroll with no crawlable path, indexable filter explosions, duplicate manufacturer copy, and categories created only for keyword variants.

## 8. Landing page

### Job

Convert a defined audience from a defined source into one primary action. Separate paid-campaign, organic, launch, event, lead magnet, and hybrid landing pages because their traffic expectations differ.

### Research emphasis

- traffic source, ad/email/social message, audience awareness, device, offer, and conversion event;
- query intent for organic/hybrid pages;
- customer language, objections, form friction, and prior tests;
- relevant campaign competitors, not merely high-ranking domain homepages.

### Default architecture

1. Message-matched headline, offer, audience, and CTA.
2. Outcome and reason to act.
3. What is included/how it works.
4. Proof placed near the claim it supports.
5. Objections, risk reversal, eligibility, price/commitment, and exclusions.
6. Repeat CTA/form with clear post-submit expectation.

### Conversion rules

Keep one primary conversion. Remove navigation only when it helps the campaign and does not trap users or reduce needed trust. Ask only for fields required at this stage. Treat copy length as a hypothesis based on awareness, risk, and evidence—not a universal benchmark.

### SEO/technical notes

Decide whether the page is an enduring organic asset, a hybrid page, or a short-lived/duplicated campaign page. Apply canonical/noindex intentionally; do not index many near-identical ad variants. Preserve analytics parameters without creating duplicate indexable URLs.

### Common failures

Message mismatch, multiple competing CTAs, unqualified lead magnets, fake countdowns, hidden conditions, long forms before value, fabricated social proof, and copying a winning case-study length or uplift as a rule.

## 9. Pricing

### Job

Help prospects understand whether they can afford, compare, and select the right plan, package, or pricing process—and what they will actually pay or need to provide for a quote.

### Research emphasis

- pricing, cost, plan, package, fee, calculator, comparison, trial, and contract queries;
- plan selection, sales objections, churn/refund reasons, support tickets, and quote friction;
- billing period, currency, tax, usage limits, overages, add-ons, setup, cancellation, renewal, and eligibility;
- competitor transparency and packaging, without price collusion or copying.

### Default architecture

1. Pricing model and who each option is for.
2. Price/billing basis or a transparent quote process.
3. Plans/packages with inclusions, limits, and primary CTA.
4. Comparison table focused on decision differences.
5. Add-ons, usage/overage, taxes, setup, discounts, contract, cancellation, refunds, and renewal where applicable.
6. Recommendation aid or calculator when reliable.
7. Proof and pricing-specific questions.
8. Enterprise/custom route and contact expectation.

### Conversion rules

Let users self-select. “Contact for price” must explain what changes the price and what happens next. Do not use a misleading starting price, hidden mandatory fee, false “most popular” label, or inaccessible comparison table.

### Common failures

Ambiguous units, monthly/annual confusion, omitted limits, unclear currency/tax, hiding cancellation, unlabeled discounts, fake scarcity, and feature tables too dense to compare.

## 10. Physical-location and service-area pages

### Job

Answer whether the organization genuinely operates at or serves a place, what is available there, and how the visitor should act. A physical branch and a service-only area are different page types.

### Research emphasis

- verified address/storefront eligibility, hours, phone, departments, accessibility, directions, parking, appointment/walk-in rules, and local inventory/services;
- true service coverage, dispatch/delivery constraints, fees, and operating base;
- local queries, Local Pack/GBP, organic results, reviews, official local requirements, and real local work;
- URL and city/service multiplication across the entire site.

### Physical-location architecture

1. Exact location/business name and available service.
2. Address, map/directions, hours, phone, appointment/action.
3. Services/products/departments at that specific location.
4. Access, parking, landmarks, transport, or entry details.
5. Real local team, imagery, reviews, and work where permitted.
6. Policies, eligibility, and nearby alternatives.

### Service-area architecture

1. State the real operating base and that the business serves the target area on site/remotely/delivery as applicable.
2. Clarify real coverage, service scope, constraints, fees, and next step.
3. Add a substantive, verified decision aid or evidence relevant to that visitor.
4. Link to the real location/hub and core service pages.

### Hard gate

For every service-area URL, require real coverage, no intent collision, a navigable hierarchy, and useful standalone value beyond swapping the place name. If the page only funnels to a generic service or fabricates proximity, recommend `MERGE` or `HOLD`.

### Schema/local rules

Use `LocalBusiness` only for a real eligible location and its true details. Do not create fake addresses, virtual-office branches, separate map profiles, or city-specific entities for service-only areas. `areaServed` does not create a branch or overcome distance.

### Common failures

False “local office” language, city-swap copy, fake map pins, invented local hazards, incorrect project labels, bulk city × service pages, and promises of local-pack ranking.

## 11. FAQ or help hub

### Job

Resolve recurring questions efficiently, route users to the right detailed page, and reduce support or purchase friction. It should not exist merely to collect long-tail keywords.

### Research emphasis

- support/sales questions, PAA, onsite search, community language, returns, objections, and failed tasks;
- whether the question belongs on a service/product/pricing/contact page instead;
- content freshness, ownership, escalation, and policy sources.

### Default architecture

1. Group questions by user task or stage, not internal department jargon.
2. Use descriptive question headings and direct answers first.
3. Add conditions, steps, source links, and contact/escalation only where needed.
4. Link to the canonical detailed page rather than duplicate it.
5. Show last review date when answers are time-sensitive.

### SEO/schema notes

FAQ rich results stopped appearing in Google Search on 7 May 2026. `FAQPage` may remain valid schema.org vocabulary, but do not promise a Google rich result or AI citation. Use markup only when it truthfully represents visible FAQ content and remains useful for another system.

### Common failures

Dozens of keyword variants, vague answers that force contact, hiding policy terms, duplicating product/service copy, unsupported safety answers, stale information, and assuming schema creates visibility.

## 12. Case study or customer story

### Job

Help a similar prospect evaluate capability through a truthful, permissioned account of a real situation, constraints, work, and outcome. It is evidence, not a disguised sales fantasy.

### Research/evidence emphasis

- written permission, confidentiality, name/logo/quote approvals, and asset rights;
- baseline, timeframe, challenge, constraints, approach, deliverables, outcome, measurement method, attribution, and caveats;
- target industries/use cases and questions buyers use to find evidence;
- related service/product/location pages and competitor evidence patterns.

### Default architecture

1. Outcome/context summary with accurate timeframe and scope.
2. Customer/situation and qualification for relevance.
3. Problem, baseline, constraints, and stakes.
4. Approach and why decisions were made.
5. Implementation/deliverables and collaboration.
6. Results with definitions, data source, timeframe, and limitations.
7. Approved customer quote and attribution.
8. What this demonstrates—and what it does not.
9. CTA to the relevant service/product or consultation.

### Conversion rules

Lead with relevance and evidence, not a long company introduction. Provide filters/listing summaries on a case-study hub so prospects can find stories by industry, use case, product, or location.

### Common failures

Invented clients, unnamed results that imply more than they prove, percentage uplifts without baseline/timeframe, taking full credit for multi-factor outcomes, fabricated quotes, re-labeling stock/AI media as project evidence, and publishing confidential details.
