# Research Workflow for Commercial Website Pages

Use this reference before drafting. The default is a full research package; reduce scope only when the user explicitly asks for a faster or narrower deliverable.

## 1. Research setup

Record:

- website, page type, current/proposed URL, task date, and owner request;
- target country, region/city, language, device, and audience;
- business model, offer, awareness stage, primary conversion, and qualified outcome;
- current page owner for the intent and possible overlap;
- tools, accounts, paid datasets, browser/search localization, and access limits.

Do not call a generic web search “local SERP research” unless the location and language are controlled or verified. Label exploratory results accurately.

## 2. First-party evidence and page inventory

For an existing site, inspect the homepage, About, Contact, relevant service/product/category/location pages, navigation, footer, policies, and structured-data/entity signals. When access exists, use:

- GSC page/query/country/device data;
- analytics landing-page and conversion data;
- CRM, call, form, sales, support, and onsite-search language;
- Merchant Center, GBP, product feed, inventory, returns/shipping, or business hours;
- approved reviews, case studies, photos, documentation, and stakeholder interviews.

Differentiate real first-party performance from third-party estimates. Missing analytics is a measurement gap, not permission to relabel an SEO tool's traffic estimate as actual traffic.

Create a `URL → intent → conversion → status` map. Check live, draft, archived, redirected, canonicalized, and parameter/facet URLs where relevant. A new page must have a distinct role.

## 3. Keyword and intent research

Build seeds from the product/service/category, user problem, outcome, use case, audience, location, urgency, comparison, alternative, cost, trust, brand, and next-step language.

Use the best available sources in this order:

1. GSC and first-party onsite/sales/support language.
2. Current SERP features, autocomplete, related searches, PAA, shopping/local/video results.
3. Google Ads Keyword Planner or reputable SEO datasets when available.
4. Google Trends for relative direction, seasonality, and rising/related queries.
5. Reviews, Reddit, Quora, specialist forums, communities, and social/video comments for vocabulary and objections.

For every metric, record source, date, country/location, language/database, match or clustering behavior, and whether it is an estimate. Keyword Planner competition is advertiser competition, not organic ranking difficulty. A zero or “no data” result does not prove zero demand.

Classify keywords and questions by:

- intent: navigational, informational, commercial investigation, transactional, local, support;
- awareness: unaware/problem-aware/solution-aware/product-aware/most-aware;
- decision stage: discover, evaluate, validate, act, retain;
- page ownership: target page, another existing page, new supporting page, or no-index/help content;
- priority: business value, relevance, evidence, attainability, and conversion fit.

Choose a primary topic and a small number of supporting clusters. Do not create a page for every query variation. Use semantic coverage and natural language, not exact-match repetition.

## 4. Google Trends

Set the geography, time range, category, and search type appropriate to the page. Compare terms/topics in the same chart when making relative claims. Review both:

- a recent window for current movement and seasonality;
- a longer window for recurring patterns and structural change.

Inspect related and rising queries. If a city or niche has insufficient data, widen the geography and disclose it. Trends is a sampled, normalized 0–100 index, not absolute search volume; zero may mean insufficient data. Do not compare separate screenshots as though their scales are identical.

Use Trends to influence timing, examples, terminology, content refresh, inventory, and campaign messaging. Do not chase a spike unrelated to the offer or create thin pages for every rising term.

## 5. Customer-language and community research

Search the target language and local variants across Reddit, Quora, relevant forums, reviews, video comments, marketplaces, support communities, and Q&A platforms that are accessible.

Collect:

- exact question wording and situation;
- desired outcome and decision stage;
- fears, failed attempts, comparison criteria, and objections;
- unclear terminology and words users prefer;
- source URL, date, and whether the observation is isolated or repeated.

Use communities to discover questions, not to establish facts, safety instructions, legal requirements, prices, or prevalence. Do not copy personal stories, expose identities, fake participation, or create manufactured mentions. Paraphrase into useful answers supported by first-party or authoritative evidence.

Stop when new searches no longer add a meaningful intent, objection, or vocabulary pattern.

## 6. SERP inventory

Select the primary query and usually 2–4 supporting queries that represent different important intents. For each, record:

- date, target country/city, language, device, personalization/localization limits;
- Ads/LSA, Local Pack, Shopping, AIO/AI Mode, PAA, images, video, forums, and organic results separately;
- every accessible organic result on page one, not only preferred competitors;
- rank, result type, domain, URL, title/snippet, and page type;
- repeated domains, marketplaces, directories, editorial pages, and direct businesses.

The page-one count is not always ten. A guide, marketplace, directory, category, or video can reveal that Google interprets the query differently from the requested page type. If the target page conflicts with dominant intent, recommend a different page role or query cluster.

## 7. Competitor page analysis

Open every accessible organic page in the approved sample. Do not judge from snippets alone. Review desktop/mobile evidence when available:

- intent and audience fit;
- title, H1, opening answer/value proposition, and page depth;
- section architecture and order;
- products/services/use cases/selection help;
- questions, objections, price/cost, scope, exclusions, process, and next step;
- proof: named experts, customer evidence, data, demonstrations, specifications, photos/video, reviews, policies;
- conversion path, CTA wording, forms, contact choices, and response expectations;
- entity clarity, locations, authorship where expected, freshness, and consistency;
- internal links, breadcrumbs, media, indexability, and structured data when inspectable;
- accessibility, scanability, mobile friction, and performance observations;
- unsupported claims, missing answers, duplicated filler, and misleading patterns.

For each competitor, log `strength with evidence`, `gap with evidence`, `table stake`, `opportunity`, and `do not copy`. Separate content quality from domain authority, backlinks, brand demand, proximity, feed eligibility, reviews, and other possible causes of visibility.

Use competitor length as context, not a target. Recommend the shortest page that resolves the required decision with adequate evidence. If a short conversion page and a detailed organic page are both defensible, present both options and ask the user.

## 8. AI-search testing

Test only where a live AI-search surface is available. For a full page brief, start with 6–12 prompts adjusted to the decision complexity:

- unbranded category/service/product request;
- problem or use-case request;
- comparison or “best fit” request;
- price, plan, shipping, location, or availability request;
- trust, evidence, or risk objection;
- branded fact-check request;
- natural follow-up that adds a constraint.

Do not mention the brand in discovery prompts and then count its appearance as discovery. Record platform, model/mode when visible, search enabled/disabled, date, locale, prompt, response, cited URLs, mentioned brands, accuracy, and whether the target page was retrieved/cited.

Repeat a representative subset because outputs vary. Distinguish:

- brand mention without citation;
- target-domain citation;
- specific-page citation;
- recommendation as an option;
- accurate factual answer;
- referral or conversion.

Do not infer market share from a small prompt set. An AI answer may use query fan-out or sources different from the ordinary organic top ten. Improve retrievability with clear page topics, relevant question coverage, crawlability, accurate entity/product data, and original evidence—not FAQ stuffing, fake mentions, tiny “AI chunks,” or invented schema.

If testing is unavailable, say `NOT TESTED`; do not fabricate likely answers.

## 9. Synthesis and stopping rules

Create four layers:

1. `TABLE STAKES`: information users and the SERP consistently require.
2. `DIFFERENTIATORS`: verified first-party value, proof, or decision support competitors lack.
3. `RISKS/HOLDS`: claims, legal issues, duplication, intent mismatch, or missing evidence.
4. `PAGE MAP`: what belongs on this page, elsewhere on the site, or nowhere.

Research is sufficient when:

- the page's intent and owner are clear;
- priority queries and customer questions are mapped;
- page-one competitors were inventoried and accessible pages inspected;
- Trends/community sources no longer add a material pattern;
- every major promise has evidence or conservative wording;
- the section order and conversion path can be justified;
- research limitations are recorded.

Do not keep searching merely to increase source count.
